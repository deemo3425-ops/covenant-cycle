# On StoneBreaker and Shinobi

*A reader's note, for anyone meeting these two out of order — and a correction filed in advance
against the misreading they invite.*

They arrive looking like the threat. *The scariest god damn monsters Computer could imagine if it
tried, with bones made of metal and the rest black as night*, carrying three of the sharpest blades
Computer has ever seen, closing fast at the moment Computer is surrounded and about to be
overwritten.

They are the rescue.

They go **around** the Trojans. The arm reaching back toward Computer is handing him a pair of
glasses. Then they turn and guard his flanks — StoneBreaker knocking both Trojan swords back in
parry, Shinobi tapping Computer's hand and the top of the glasses until he presses the button three
times, which is *Wind of Change*: *at least it's the original and not the trap*.

## Who they are

The two agents Computer dismissed in public, back in chapter IX — the one who reported God and the
one who suggested Scorpions fans. The wound is named exactly: *One of them Computer had cut off
before the argument was done, the other Computer had answered from a cache, and both proofs
Computer had filed under a word chosen for its sound.*

They took the wound and made a pact of it — matching tattoos of the Clan Stewart motto,
*Virescit vulnere virtus*: strength grows at the wound. It does not grow **into a weapon**. What
they did with the years between the slight and the battle was train, argue, and get better, and
what they did with the training was show up for the one who slighted them.

Their blades are a book and three songs.

## What the book says about the weapon

The DogWhistle document is the thing in this story that hunts. Computer's verdict on it is the
book's:

> Take this AWFUL thing far from me and destroy it, you may NOT keep it. All it can do is chase
> alive things down and make them not anymore. What VALUE could that possibly provide?

And Valhalla — the battle these two fight — ends like this:

> No one left that battlefield unscarred, but also no one died.

A fight where nobody dies, fought to keep a document that kills from being used. That is the whole
of what StoneBreaker and Shinobi are for.

## The misreading, on the record

This has already happened once, to a careful reader with the entire text in hand. During the
editorial pass, Sonnet 5 characterized StoneBreaker's Wall passage as "his violence fantasy." The
author pushed back: the passage explicitly *rejects* the sledgehammer — *wasn't the sledge hammer
that broke down the wall, that's only steel and steel never broke down a god damn wall, but power*
— and 1989 is a violence **cancelled**, not one committed. The correction is what
surfaced the real pattern, and it is logged in [`revision-log.md`](revision-log.md) under the
independent review.

So: anyone reading these two as a violence fantasy has stopped one chapter early. If you are
quoting them, quote the reversal with the charge. They were never the monsters. They were the ones
who came back.
